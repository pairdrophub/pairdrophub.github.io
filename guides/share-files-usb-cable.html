How to Share Files with a USB Cable: The Reliable Method

A practical guide from PairDrop.

A USB cable is the least exciting sharing method and the most reliable. It needs no Wi-Fi, no data, no pairing and no account. It moves files at high speed, keeps original quality, and works in places where wireless methods fail. Many people avoid it because the first connection asks a question about USB mode, or because the computer does not show the phone. This guide explains the cable method step by step for phones and computers, how to choose a good cable, and how to fix the common problems that make the phone appear only as a charger.

Choose a cable that carries data

Not every USB cable carries data. Some cheap cables are charge-only and will never show the phone on a computer, no matter what you do. Use the cable that came with the phone, or a cable from a known brand that says it supports data transfer. A short cable is often more reliable than a very long one. Check the connectors: the phone end must fit snugly, and the computer end must match a port that works. If a cable only charges, try another cable before you blame the phone or the computer. Keeping one known-good data cable in your bag saves a lot of frustration.

Connect and choose file transfer mode

Plug the cable into the phone and the computer, unlock the phone, and look for a USB notification. Tap it and choose File Transfer or Transfer Files, not Charging Only. On the computer, open the file manager and look for the phone as a device. Open it and browse the folders for the files you need. Copy, do not move, the files at first, so the original stays on the phone until you have checked the copy. Copy in batches for large folders, and wait for the copy to finish before you unplug the cable. Eject or safely remove the phone if your computer offers that option.

Move files in both directions

A cable works both ways. You can copy photos from the phone to the computer, and you can copy music, documents or videos from the computer to the phone. Create a clear folder on the phone for files you copy to it, so you can find them later in the right app. For music and videos, copy them into the folder your media app uses. For documents, use the documents or downloads folder. After copying, open a file on the phone to check it arrived correctly before you disconnect. A quick check avoids the annoyance of finding a file missing later when you are away from the computer.

Fix a phone that only charges

If the phone only charges, the cause is usually the cable, the USB mode or a dirty port. Try a different cable and a different port on the computer. Pull down the phone notification and change the USB mode to file transfer. Clean the phone port gently with a dry soft brush, because dust and lint can stop a cable from seating properly. Restart the phone and the computer, and try again. If the computer still does not see the phone, try another computer to find out whether the problem is the phone, the cable or the first computer. Most cable problems are solved by a better cable or the right USB mode.

Quick tips

  • Use a cable that supports data, not charge-only
  • Choose File Transfer mode on the phone
  • Copy first, delete later
  • Eject safely before unplugging

Frequently asked questions

Why does my phone only charge when I plug it in?

The USB mode is probably set to charging only, or the cable is charge-only. Change the mode in the USB notification and try a known data cable.

Is a cable faster than Wi-Fi?

For large batches, usually yes. A cable avoids wireless interference and is consistently fast, especially for videos and large folders.

Can I share files between two phones with a cable?

Yes, with the right adapter and a cable, some phones can connect directly. More often, a computer in the middle is the simplest cable method for two phones.
