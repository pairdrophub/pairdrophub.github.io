Share Files with Nearby Share and Quick Share Explained

A practical guide from PairDrop.

Nearby Share, now called Quick Share on many Android phones, is the fast wireless tool built into Android for sending files to devices close by. It uses Bluetooth to find the other device and Wi-Fi to move the file quickly, so it is much faster than Bluetooth alone and needs no internet. This guide explains how it works, how to turn it on, how to send and receive files, and how to control who can see your device. It also covers what to do when a nearby device does not appear, which is the most common problem people meet with this tool.

How nearby sharing works

Nearby sharing combines two radios. Bluetooth discovers the other device and agrees the connection, then Wi-Fi carries the file at high speed. The file goes directly from one device to the other and is not uploaded to a server. That is why it is fast and private, and why it works with no mobile data. Both devices need Bluetooth and Wi-Fi turned on, even though no internet is needed. Location may also need to be on, because the phone uses it to find nearby devices. Once you understand this combination, the settings make sense and troubleshooting becomes much easier.

Send a file in a few taps

Open the file you want to send, tap Share, and choose Nearby Share or Quick Share. The phone will search for nearby devices. Tap the device name when it appears, and the other person taps Accept. The file transfers quickly, and both phones show a confirmation when it is done. For photos, you can select several at once and send them in one batch. Keep both phones unlocked and close together until the transfer finishes. If you send files to the same person often, their device will appear quickly after the first successful transfer.

Control who can discover you

By default, nearby sharing can be set so that only your contacts can see you, only your own devices can see you, or everyone nearby can see you for a short time. For everyday use, contacts-only is a sensible choice. Everyone mode is useful at an event or in a classroom, but turn it back to contacts-only afterwards. If you do not want to receive files from strangers, never leave discovery on everyone mode in a public place. You can also turn the feature off entirely when you are not using it. These small choices keep a handy tool from becoming an unwanted invitation.

Fix a device that does not appear

If the other device does not show up, check that Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and location are on for both devices, that both screens are unlocked, and that the devices are within a few metres. Turn the feature off and on again, and check the visibility setting on the receiving device. A VPN can interfere, so turn it off during the transfer. Restarting both phones fixes many discovery problems. If one device is a computer, make sure the matching sharing app or feature is installed and running, because a phone alone cannot always see a laptop without it.

Quick tips

  • Turn on Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and location together
  • Use contacts-only visibility by default
  • Keep phones unlocked and close
  • Turn off the VPN during the transfer

Frequently asked questions

Do I need internet for Nearby Share?

No. It uses Bluetooth to find the device and Wi-Fi to move the file directly. No mobile data or internet connection is needed.

Can I share with a computer?

Yes, if the computer has the matching sharing feature or app installed and running. Check that both devices are signed in or set to visible to each other.

Is Nearby Share private?

The file goes directly between devices and is not uploaded. Use contacts-only visibility so strangers nearby cannot send you files.
